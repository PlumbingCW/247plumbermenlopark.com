How to Repair a Broken Faucet in Menlo Park, CA

Most faucet repairs come down to identifying the type, swapping one part and putting it back together. Here is how.

By the Menlo Park 24/7 Plumbing team · 2026

A broken faucet in Menlo Park, CA homes is usually one of four problems: a drip from the spout, a leak at the handle, weak flow or a handle that is loose or stiff. Each traces to a small part such as a cartridge, an O-ring, a washer or an aerator, and all of them cost less than a new faucet.

Before you start, close the shutoff valves under the sink. If you cannot close them, shut the main water valve and call (650) 860-1670 rather than force a stuck valve.

Step One: Identify Your Faucet Type

Four faucet types and the part that usually fails
TypeHow to spot itUsual failure
Compression.Two handles that you turn several times.Rubber washer or seat.
Cartridge.One handle or two, smooth motion.Cartridge or O-ring.
Ball.Single handle over a rounded cap, common on kitchen faucets.Seals and springs.
Ceramic disc.Single lever with a wide cylinder body.Seals or the disc cartridge.

Tools and Parts to Gather

  • Adjustable wrench and a set of screwdrivers.
  • Hex keys, since many handles use a set screw.
  • Plumber's grease for new O-rings.
  • A towel and a small bowl for screws.
  • Replacement parts matched to the brand and model, bring the old part to compare.

A Basic Cartridge Faucet Repair

  1. Close the water

    Turn the shutoff valves under the sink clockwise and open the faucet to drain pressure.

  2. Remove the handle

    Pry off the cap, remove the screw and lift the handle off the stem.

  3. Pull the cartridge

    Remove the retaining nut or clip and pull the cartridge straight out. Note its orientation.

  4. Replace and reassemble

    Install the new cartridge, apply grease to O-rings, reassemble and open the shutoffs slowly.

  5. Test for leaks

    Run the faucet hot and cold and check under the sink for drips.

Leaks at the Base or the Handle

A leak around the handle usually means a worn O-ring or a loose packing nut. A leak at the base of a kitchen spout points to worn spout O-rings. Both are inexpensive parts, and a kit often covers them all.

If the leak is under the sink, look at the supply lines and the shutoff valve stem before blaming the faucet. A seized or leaking shutoff valve is a plumbing repair, not a faucet repair, and our faucet repair team replaces them.

Weak Flow and Low Pressure

Remove the aerator, the small screen at the tip of the spout, and rinse it. Fine sediment collects there, especially after work on a nearby water main. If flow is still weak, check that both shutoff valves are fully open and look for kinks in the supply lines.

Low pressure at one fixture is a faucet issue. Low pressure at every fixture is a house or service line issue that may involve the pressure regulator or a leak.

When to Replace Instead of Repair

Replace the faucet if the body is cracked, the finish is peeling, parts are no longer made or you have repaired the same faucet twice in a year. A new faucet also lets you choose a model with a pull-down sprayer or a better flow rate.

If you are fitting a new fixture, our plumbing installation page covers sinks, faucets and supply lines, and we can handle shutoffs that need replacing at the same time.

Kitchen Faucets With Pull-Down Sprayers

Pull-down and pull-out sprayers add a hose, a weight and a quick-connect fitting under the sink. When the spray head dribbles or the hose will not retract, the usual causes are a kinked hose, a missing counterweight or mineral buildup in the spray head. Unscrewing the head and rinsing the screen often fixes weak spray.

If water leaks from the sprayer hose connection under the sink, the quick-connect fitting may be loose or the hose may be worn. Both can be replaced without removing the faucet.

Bathroom Faucets and Pop-Up Drains

Bathroom sink problems often involve the pop-up drain assembly rather than the faucet. A stopper that does not seal, a slow drain from hair on the pivot rod or a leak at the nut under the sink all belong to the drain, not the tap. A short length of replacement tailpiece and a new gasket usually settle it.

If the faucet leaks only when the stopper is closed, check the overflow and the gaskets at the pop-up body.

Safety and Care Before You Start

  • Place a towel in the sink so small parts cannot fall into the drain.
  • Take a photo of each step so you can reassemble in order.
  • Turn the faucet on after closing the shutoffs to relieve pressure.
  • Do not force a stuck handle or nut, penetrating oil and patience work better.
  • Open the shutoff valves slowly after the repair to avoid a surge.
