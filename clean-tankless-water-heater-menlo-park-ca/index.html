Cleaning a Tankless Water Heater in Menlo Park, CA

A yearly flush keeps a tankless unit running well. Here is the process and what it protects.

By the Menlo Park 24/7 Plumbing team · 2026

Cleaning a tankless water heater means flushing its heat exchanger with a descaling solution and clearing the small filter at the cold water inlet. In Menlo Park, CA homes it is still worth doing, because even soft water leaves some mineral film on a heat exchanger and a clogged filter lowers flow enough to cut the burner off.

This guide covers the tools, the steps and the signs that the unit needs a pro. If your heater shows an error code or delivers cold water, call (650) 860-1670.

Why Tankless Units Need Cleaning

Water passing through the heat exchanger leaves dissolved minerals behind. Over time these form scale that narrows the passages, slows heat transfer and forces the unit to work harder. Scale is also a common reason for error codes tied to temperature or flow.

Even low-scale water can cause trouble if the unit is neglected for years. Following the maker's schedule is the safest approach.

What You Need

  • A small submersible pump with hoses, usually sold in tankless flush kits.
  • A five-gallon bucket.
  • White vinegar or a descaling solution recommended by the maker.
  • Two or three gallons of water for rinsing.
  • A towel, a screwdriver and the unit's manual.
  • Isolation valves, which are installed on the water lines to the unit, if you do not have them yet.

The Flush, Step by Step

  1. Turn off power and gas

    Switch off the unit and close the gas shutoff, and unplug it if it has a cord.

  2. Close the isolation valves

    Close the hot and cold isolation valves and open the service ports.

  3. Connect the pump

    Attach one hose to the cold side service port and one to the hot side, with both ends in the bucket.

  4. Circulate the solution

    Add the vinegar or descaler and run the pump for the time the maker recommends, often about an hour.

  5. Rinse and reassemble

    Drain the bucket, flush with clean water, close the ports, open the valves and restore power and gas.

  6. Clean the inlet filter

    Remove and rinse the small screen at the cold water inlet.

How Often to Flush

Typical flush guidance by water type
WaterTypical approach
Hard water.Often once a year or more, follow the maker.
Moderate hardness.Around once a year.
Soft water such as much of Menlo Park.The maker's schedule still applies, many suggest at least every year or two.
Well water or mixed supply.Test the water and shorten the interval.

Signs It Is Time

Error codes

Codes tied to flow, temperature or ignition can come from scale or a clogged filter.

Weak hot water flow

A dirty inlet screen or scaled heat exchanger can cut flow.

Cold bursts

Flow that drops below the minimum can cause the burner to cycle off.

Noises

Rumbling or kettle sounds suggest scale on the heat exchanger.

Cloudy water or white flakes

Mineral flakes in the filter show buildup.

When to Call a Plumber Instead

Call if you do not have isolation valves, if the unit is gas and you are uncomfortable working near the supply or if the unit gives a venting or ignition code after a flush. A plumber can add isolation valves, flush the unit and test combustion in one visit.

Cleaning the Inlet Filter and Checking Aerators

The inlet screen is a small mesh near the cold water connection, and it catches grit that would otherwise reach the heat exchanger. Turn off the water, unscrew the filter housing, pull out the screen and rinse it under a faucet. Reinstall it carefully so the seal sits flat.

Also remove and rinse the aerators on faucets that seem to have weak hot water flow. A clogged aerator can look like a heater problem when the heater is fine.

Mistakes That Damage a Tankless Unit

  • Using a descaler the maker does not allow.
  • Running the pump without enough solution to cover the lines.
  • Forgetting to open the isolation valves after the flush.
  • Leaving the gas supply closed and wondering why there is no heat.
  • Skipping the rinse, which leaves vinegar in the lines.

Tracking Maintenance

Write the date of each flush on a label next to the unit or in a notes app. Many makers ask for proof of maintenance if a warranty claim comes up. A record also helps a plumber diagnose a problem, since it shows how long the unit has gone without service.

If your tankless heater serves a large household, a pre-filter on the supply line can reduce sediment before it reaches the unit, and a plumber can fit one when isolation valves are added.

Menlo Park Local Note: What Menlo Park Water Does to a Heat Exchanger

California Water Service's Bear Gulch district reports an average hardness of 46 ppm in its 2024 report, for water it purchased from the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission. The SFPUC regional report lists an average of 60 ppm, with a range from 8.4 to 106 ppm. These numbers describe soft water, so scale builds more slowly than in much of California.

Your own address may be served by Menlo Park Municipal Water, Cal Water or O'Connor Tract, and each reports separately. Ask your provider, and then follow the heater maker's schedule rather than skipping flushes altogether.
