A closed restaurant or an office with no water costs money every hour. We work around your hours and explain the price first.

Grease Traps and Interceptors

Kitchens produce fat, oil and grease, and a grease trap or interceptor catches it before it reaches the sewer. A full trap lets grease pass into the line, where it hardens and blocks the pipe. Cleaning schedules depend on the size of the trap and the amount of cooking. Many local rules require traps to be cleaned and documented.

Ask your sewer district what it requires for food service. The plumber can service the trap and keep the line downstream clear so the backups do not return.

Backflow Devices and Testing

A backflow preventer keeps dirty water from flowing backward into the public supply. Many commercial properties, as well as irrigation systems and fire lines, have one, and water providers usually require annual testing by a certified tester. Menlo Park Municipal Water, California Water Service and O'Connor Tract Co-operative Water each serve different parts of the city, so check which one bills you before scheduling the test.

A failed device is repaired or replaced and retested. Keep the test report. The provider may ask for it.

Storm Drains and Parking Areas

Private storm drains in lots, loading areas and courtyards clog with leaves and debris. When the San Francisquito Creek and the Atherton Channel rose on December 31, 2022 and flooding reached El Camino Real, Alma Street, Laurel Street and Middlefield Road, many private drains were already near capacity. We clear private storm drain lines and catch basins and recommend a pre-winter check.

Street inlets and public lines belong to the city, so report those to Menlo Park Public Works.
