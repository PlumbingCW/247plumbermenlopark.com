Water Heater Gas Control Valve Replacement in Menlo Park, CA

The gas control valve is the part that tells the burner when to run. When it fails, the heater goes cold, and the safest fix is a professional one.

By the Menlo Park 24/7 Plumbing team · 2026

A water heater gas control valve is the combination thermostat and gas valve at the base of a tank heater. It senses water temperature, opens gas to the burner and shuts it off when the pilot or igniter fails. Water heater gas control valve replacement in Menlo Park, CA is a common reason for a no-hot-water call, and it is also work that involves gas, so it belongs to a licensed plumber.

If you smell gas at any point, leave the house first, then call 911 and PG&E at 1-800-743-5000 from outside. Do not call us first and do not touch switches or appliances.

Signs the Control Valve Is Failing

Pilot will not stay lit

A weak thermocouple is the first suspect, but a failing valve can also drop the pilot.

Burner does not ignite

The pilot is lit, but the burner never fires when the water cools. The valve may not be opening.

Water too hot or too cold

The thermostat in the valve may be reading or setting temperature wrong.

Gas smell near the unit

Leave and call. A leaking valve is a safety issue, not a repair project.

Dial is loose or cracked

A physical failure of the dial or its mount calls for replacement.

Why This Is Not a Good DIY Job

Replacing a gas control valve means disconnecting a gas line, draining part of the tank, rethreading connections and testing for leaks with a proper solution or gauge. Gas threads must be sealed with the right compound and checked every time. A small mistake can leave a slow leak that no one notices.

Parts are also model-specific. A valve that fits a different brand may not match the thermocouple, the manifold or the burner, so the plumber sources the right one for your unit.

What a Replacement Visit Includes

  1. Diagnose

    The plumber tests the thermocouple, pilot and gas supply to confirm the valve is at fault.

  2. Shut off and drain

    Gas and water are shut off, and the tank is partly drained.

  3. Replace the valve

    The old valve is removed and a matching valve installed with new seals.

  4. Leak test and relight

    Gas connections are checked, the heater is relit and the temperature set.

  5. Check venting

    The flue draft and the relief valve are inspected before the plumber leaves.

Repair or Replace the Whole Heater?

A control valve on a heater under about ten years old is usually worth replacing. On an older tank that is rusting, leaking or has had other repairs, the money is better spent on a new unit. A new gas valve does not stop a tank from corroding.

If the answer is a new tank, see our water heater replacement page. If it is the valve or another part, water heater repair covers what we fix.

Safety Checks While the Plumber Is There

  • Check the flue for proper draft and secure joints.
  • Confirm there is a working temperature and pressure relief valve and a discharge pipe.
  • Look for a sediment buildup that may be causing noise or overheating.
  • Make sure seismic strapping is in place and ask the building department if it is required.
  • Check clearance from combustible items stored near the heater.

How Gas Control Valves Fail

Gas control valves wear from heat cycles, sediment in the tank that overheats the sensor and moisture in the burner compartment. Some fail all at once and others drift, so the water gets lukewarm for weeks before the pilot dies. A heater that behaves oddly after a power outage or a gas supply interruption may have damaged valve electronics.

Sediment buildup at the bottom of a tank can also trip a high-limit switch and mimic a valve failure. A plumber checks the limit switch, the thermocouple and the supply pressure before pointing at the valve.

Questions to Ask Before You Approve the Work

  • Is the part an exact match for this heater model, and is it new, not used.
  • Does the quote include a gas leak test and relighting.
  • How old is the tank and what does its anode and bottom look like.
  • Would you recommend replacing the whole unit at this age, and why.
  • Is there a warranty on the part and the labor.

Pilot Light Habits That Prevent Service Calls

Keep the area around the heater clear so combustion air can reach the burner. Do not store paint, solvents or cleaning supplies beside it, since fumes can ignite or damage the sensor. Check the pilot flame now and then. A steady blue flame is normal. A yellow or flickering flame can signal poor combustion or a dirty burner.

Every year or two, ask the plumber who visits to check the flue draft and the relief valve. A small service visit costs far less than an emergency call at night.
