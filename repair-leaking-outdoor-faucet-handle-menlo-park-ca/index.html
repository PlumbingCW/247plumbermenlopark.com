Repair a Leaking Outdoor Faucet Handle in Menlo Park, CA

A leak behind the handle is almost always a worn packing or washer. The fix takes minutes and a few cheap parts.

By the Menlo Park 24/7 Plumbing team · 2026

An outdoor faucet, also called a hose bib or spigot, leaks around the handle when the packing around the stem wears out or the packing nut loosens. Water then seeps out behind the handle each time it is turned on. Repairing a leaking outdoor faucet handle in Menlo Park, CA usually takes a wrench, a screwdriver and a replacement washer or packing.

If water is dripping inside the wall or the faucet cannot be shut off, close the main and call (650) 860-1670. A leak that runs for weeks can rot siding and framing.

Why Outdoor Faucets Leak at the Handle

Loose packing nut

The nut behind the handle presses packing around the stem. If it loosens, water escapes.

Worn packing or washer

Rubber hardens and cracks with age and sun.

Corroded stem

Old brass or steel stems pit, which prevents a good seal.

Freeze damage

Water left inside the faucet can crack it in cold weather, which is rare but possible here.

Hose left on

A hose under pressure keeps stress on the seals.

A Quick Fix: Tighten the Packing Nut

  1. Turn the water off

    If the faucet has an inside shutoff, close it. Otherwise close the main.

  2. Remove the handle

    Take out the screw in the center and lift off the handle.

  3. Find the packing nut

    The nut is behind the handle, around the stem.

  4. Tighten gently

    Tighten a quarter turn with a wrench. Do not overtighten.

  5. Test

    Turn the water on and check for seepage while the faucet runs.

Replacing the Packing or Washer

If tightening does not stop the leak, unscrew the packing nut completely and slide off the stem. Look for the old packing, a small ring or string, and replace it with new packing or an O-ring of the same size. Graphite packing string is sold at hardware stores.

If the water drips from the spout when the faucet is closed, the washer on the end of the stem is worn. Replace it with a matching washer and the retaining screw.

Choosing a New Outdoor Faucet

A frost-free sillcock has a long stem that puts the shutoff inside the heated wall, which reduces the risk of freezing. A vacuum breaker, often built into the spout, keeps garden water from flowing back into the supply, and local rules or the maker may call for one.

A plumber can also add an inside shutoff and a backflow device if you want a more complete fix. See our faucet repair page for what we handle.

Keeping Water Off the Siding

A slow leak behind the handle can soak the wall beneath it. Check the siding and the stucco around the faucet for stains, soft spots or mold. If you see damage, take photos and have a contractor check the wall once the leak is fixed.

A faucet that leaks inside the wall is more urgent. If water shows in a crawlspace or inside the house near the faucet, call the 24/7 line.

Vacuum Breakers and Backflow Protection

A hose left attached to a faucet and placed in a bucket or a pool can draw dirty water back into the supply if pressure drops. A vacuum breaker on the spout prevents that. Many outdoor faucets have one built in, and screw-on versions are cheap and easy to fit.

If your faucet has a small cap or a vent on the spout, that is likely the breaker. A breaker that leaks or spits water when the faucet runs may need replacing.

Hoses, Connections and Pressure

  • Use a washer in the hose connection and tighten by hand with a gentle wrench turn.
  • Remove hoses and splitters when not in use to reduce stress on the faucet.
  • Check for dripping at the hose end, which can look like a faucet leak.
  • Replace brittle hoses before they split under pressure.
  • Use a pressure regulator if you notice banging or spitting.

Safety checklist before you work on an outdoor faucet

  • Close the water supply first and open the faucet to relieve pressure.
  • Do not force a seized stem, it can snap the pipe inside the wall.
  • Keep ladders and tools away from electrical lines and outdoor outlets.
  • Wear gloves and eye protection when removing corroded parts.
  • Stop and call a plumber if water appears inside the wall or garage.

Spotting the Bigger Problems

Water that flows from the faucet even when it is off may point to a failed internal washer or seat. Water that appears inside the garage wall near the faucet means the pipe joint behind it is leaking. Both are plumber jobs.

If your water bill is higher than usual and no one has been watering, check the outdoor faucets. A steady drip adds up over a month.
