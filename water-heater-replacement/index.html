Water Heater Replacement in Menlo Park, CA

When a tank fails, you need hot water back and a price you understand. We explain what to buy, what to ask the city and when the work can be done.

Water heater replacement in Menlo Park, CA means removing a failed or aging unit and setting a new one in its place with new connections, a relief valve, a vent or exhaust and seismic strapping. Most tank heaters are replaced for one of three reasons: a leak from the tank body, rusty water or a unit that has passed the age where repairs make sense.

If your tank is leaking now, close the cold water valve above it, turn off gas or power and call (650) 860-1670. We can often set a replacement on the same day when a suitable unit is available.

How to Tell the Tank Is Done

  • Water pooling at the base that does not stop when the cold valve is closed.
  • Rust-colored hot water or a metallic taste.
  • Rumbling or popping from sediment burning at the bottom.
  • A tag that shows the unit is well past ten to fifteen years old.
  • Repair costs on a heater that has already needed parts recently.

What Happens on Replacement Day

  1. Shut off and drain

    The plumber closes the water and gas or power, then drains the old tank.

  2. Remove and prepare

    The old heater is disconnected and moved out. The pan and the area are checked.

  3. Set and connect

    The new unit is set, connected to the water lines, vent and gas or electric supply and fitted with a new relief valve.

  4. Strap and test

    Seismic strapping is installed, and the heater is tested for leaks, temperature and venting.

Permits and What the City Expects

Water heater replacement can involve a permit and an inspection, depending on the city and the work. Bracing a tank against earthquakes is also a safety practice, and the plumber straps the new unit. Contact the Menlo Park Building Division at 650-330-6704, or the building department in your own town, to confirm the requirements at your address.

Rules for gas appliances and the energy code change over time. Ask about current local requirements before choosing a unit, especially if you are weighing a heat pump model.

Hybrid Heat Pump Water Heaters in Menlo Park

A hybrid unit pulls heat from the surrounding air and moves it into the tank, which uses less electricity than a standard electric element. It needs clearance, a place for condensate to drain and room to breathe. A garage often works, while a small closet may not.

Menlo Park homes from the 1950s often have a garage heater location with an adjacent wall that can hold a drain line. The plumber checks the space before you buy.

Why Water Here Is Easy on a Tank

Hardness is low in this area. The Hetch Hetchy supply that serves much of Menlo Park averages about 60 ppm as calcium carbonate, and California Water Service lists 46 ppm for its customers. Soft water means less scale on elements and the tank floor, and a new tank here can often last longer than the same model in a hard water town.

Replacing the anode rod and flushing the tank at intervals still helps, and you can ask the plumber to show you how.
