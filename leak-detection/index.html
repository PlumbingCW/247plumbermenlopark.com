Emergency Leak Detection in Menlo Park, CA

Water that goes missing usually shows up first on a bill or a ceiling. We find where it goes without tearing out more than we have to.

Leak detection in Menlo Park, CA is the work of locating a hidden water leak before anything is cut open. The plumber confirms that water is escaping, narrows down the section of pipe, and then pins the spot using acoustic listening devices, thermal imaging and pressure tests. Finding it first keeps drywall, flooring and landscaping intact.

A running meter, a wet ceiling, a warm patch on the floor or a bill that suddenly climbed are all reasons to call (650) 860-1670. Closing the main stops the damage while you wait.

Start With Your Meter and Your Bill

Close every faucet, stop the washer and dishwasher, then read the meter. If the small leak indicator or the dial moves, water is leaving the system. Check again after fifteen minutes. A change tells you the leak is real, and the main shutoff test then tells you whether it is before or after the house valve.

Compare your last few water bills. A jump that does not match a visitor, a new irrigation schedule or a pool fill is worth investigating. Ask your water provider for your usage history. Menlo Park Municipal Water, California Water Service and O'Connor Tract Co-operative Water each serve different parts of the city, so check the bill for yours.

Emergency Leak Detection for Active Damage

Some leaks cannot wait. Water coming through a ceiling, soaking a wall or flooding a crawlspace needs a plumber now. Close the main, turn off power to the affected area if it is safe and call the 24/7 line. The plumber finds the source and stops it, then explains what else might be involved.

If you have a pipe that has already split, see burst pipe repair. Leak detection is the step before repair when the source is unclear.

The Tools We Use and What Each One Does

Pressure test

The plumber isolates the supply system and watches whether pressure holds. A drop confirms a leak and shows which part of the system holds it.

Acoustic listening

Ground microphones and contact sensors pick up the sound of water escaping a pressurized pipe. The signal is strongest above the leak.

Thermal imaging

A thermal camera shows temperature differences that mark hot water lines and wet material behind surfaces.

Camera inspection

For drains and sewers, a small camera shows cracks and leaks from the inside. See sewer inspection.

Water Meter Running and High Bills

Many leak calls begin with a bill, not a puddle. A leak on the service line between the meter and the house often shows up as a faint hiss, a lawn that stays wet or a meter that spins at night. A leaking toilet flapper, a dripping irrigation valve and a failing water heater relief valve are cheaper explanations worth ruling out first.

Ask your provider whether it offers a leak notice program, since some utilities will tell you if usage looks abnormal. The plumber can also check your toilets for silent leaks before looking at the pipes.

Local Causes in Menlo Park

Homes built in the 1950s in Belle Haven, the Willows, Suburban Park and Lorelei Manor, and in the 1930s in Allied Arts and Felton Gables, have supply lines that are now old enough for pinhole leaks. Sharon Heights and other hillside areas add water pressure questions that can stress fittings. The plumber checks pressure on the way in.

Rain does not usually cause supply leaks, but it can hide them. After the December 31, 2022 storm, with nearly 4 inches of rain reported in a day, wet soil made some slow leaks easy to miss. A dry-weather meter test is the cleanest check.
