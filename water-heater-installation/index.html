Water Heater Installation in Menlo Park, CA

Installation is a planned job. We talk through the location, the fuel and the code before a heater arrives on a truck.

Water heater installation in Menlo Park, CA covers new work where a heater is being added or relocated, not just swapped. That includes remodels, additions, detached units, a move from a closet to a garage and a change in fuel. The plumber plans the location, sizes the unit for the household and handles venting, drain lines and permits.

If your current heater has simply failed, our water heater replacement page is the better fit. For a planned project, call (650) 860-1670 and tell us what you are building.

Questions to Settle Before You Buy

Where will it go?

Garage, closet, attic or outdoor enclosure. Each has clearance, venting and access needs.

Which fuel?

Gas, electric or a heat pump all need different connections.

How big?

Household size, bathroom count and simultaneous use set the capacity.

Is there drainage?

A relief valve and a pan drain need a safe place to discharge.

What does the permit require?

Ask the building department, and ask whether a drawing is needed.

Remodels, ADUs and New Locations

Moving a heater from an interior closet to a garage can free up floor space, but it needs new pipe runs, a new vent path and sometimes a new gas line. Adding a detached unit or an accessory dwelling calls for separate planning because supply, drain and permits differ. Ask the building department which rules apply at your address.

Our plumbing installation page covers the wider remodel work that often comes with a heater move, such as new bath fixtures and supply lines.

Vents, Gas Lines and Electrical Needs

A gas heater needs a vent that carries exhaust outside, and a path for combustion air. An electric heater needs a dedicated circuit rated for the unit. A hybrid unit needs space for air to move and a drain for condensate. Getting any of these wrong is a safety issue.

If you smell gas at any point, leave the building, then call 911 and PG&E at 1-800-743-5000 from outside. Do not call us first.

Local Notes for Menlo Park Projects

Sharon Heights, Linfield Oaks and the Willows have many 1950s to 1970s homes where garages double as utility rooms. That often makes the garage the easiest place to site a new heater. Downtown and Allied Arts homes from the 1930s have tighter closets and older vents that may need work before a new heater can connect.

Soft water in this area, with average hardness about 60 ppm as calcium carbonate according to the Hetch Hetchy supply data, reduces scale problems on elements and tank floors compared with harder water elsewhere in California.
