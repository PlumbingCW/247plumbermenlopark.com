Whole House Water Filtration System Cost in Menlo Park, CA

The price of a filter depends on what you want to remove. Start with the water report, then choose.

By the Menlo Park 24/7 Plumbing team · 2026

The cost of a whole-house water filtration system depends on the type, the size of the home, the installation and the ongoing cartridge or media costs. A simple sediment and carbon filter sits at one end of the range. A multi-stage system with a softener or a catalytic carbon tank sits at the other. For Menlo Park, CA homes, the right system often costs less than people expect, because local water is already soft.

This guide explains the cost drivers and what local water reports show. For a price on your own house, call (650) 860-1670 and tell us what you want to improve.

What Drives the Price

Cost drivers for a whole-house filtration system

Filter type.Sediment and carbon cartridges cost less than catalytic carbon tanks or multi-stage systems.

Types of Whole-House Systems

Sediment filters

Trap sand, rust and grit. They protect pipes and appliances and are the cheapest option.

Carbon filters

Reduce chlorine taste and odor, and some organic compounds. Standard carbon works for chlorine, while chloramine usually needs catalytic carbon.

Softeners

Remove hardness minerals using salt-regenerated resin. Rarely needed in areas with soft water.

Reverse osmosis

Usually placed at one tap for drinking water rather than at the whole house, because it wastes water and has low flow.

What Menlo Park Water Already Contains

Public water systems publish annual reports on what is in the water. These reports are the right place to start, because they show what is actually there and what is already regulated. A filter chosen without them may remove nothing that matters.

If you have a specific concern, test your tap with a certified lab and compare the result to the report. Choose a system with third-party certification for the contaminant you care about.

Chloramine and Why It Matters for Filter Choice

The San Francisco Public Utilities Commission describes chloramination as its disinfection method, and its report lists a chlorine and chloramine average of 3.0 ppm with a maximum allowed level of 4.0 ppm. Chloramine is harder for ordinary carbon to remove than free chlorine, so a catalytic carbon filter is the usual choice when taste or odor is a concern.

If you want to remove chloramine at the whole house, ask for a catalytic carbon system sized for your flow, and plan for media replacement on a set schedule.

Ongoing Costs to Budget For

  • Replacement cartridges or media on the maker's schedule.
  • Pressure checks, since a clogged filter drops pressure.
  • Salt, only if a softener is part of the system.
  • Occasional service for valves and seals.
  • Water used for backwashing, where applicable.

Do You Need a Softener?

Not in most of Menlo Park. California Water Service's Bear Gulch district reports 46 ppm average hardness, and the regional SFPUC report lists about 60 ppm, which is soft water. A softener adds sodium and discharges brine for a problem that may not exist. See our water softener and filtration page for an honest look at when it does make sense.

If you see scale, check where it forms. A local issue at a water heater may have a different cause than the supply.

Getting an Accurate Quote

Ask the installer for a written scope that lists the filter type, flow rating, bypass valves, shutoffs, drain needs and the cartridge schedule. Ask what happens to your pressure when the filter is dirty.

A good quote also lists what is not included, such as electrical work or drywall. For related fixtures and new water lines, our plumbing installation page covers what we do.

Point-of-Use Versus Whole-House

A whole-house system treats all the water that enters the home, including water for showers and laundry. A point-of-use system treats one tap, usually the kitchen, where you drink and cook. Many people find that a good under-sink filter and a catalytic carbon cartridge on the main line cover their needs at a lower total cost.

If your concern is drinking water taste, start at the kitchen tap. If you are worried about chlorine on skin or hair, a whole-house carbon unit is a reasonable option.

How Long Systems Last

  • Sediment cartridges often need changing every few months.
  • Carbon cartridges last longer but lose effectiveness as they fill.
  • Catalytic carbon media lasts years but still needs replacing.
  • Housings, valves and seals can last a decade or more with care.
  • Keep a log of changes so you know when the next one is due.

Installation Considerations

The installer needs a place on the main line after the shutoff and before the first branch, with room for the housing and for removing cartridges. A bypass lets you take the filter out of service without shutting off water to the house. Most garages and utility rooms work.

A pressure gauge before and after the filter shows when pressure drops, which tells you when to replace the cartridge. Ask for one.

Menlo Park Local Note: What the Reports Say

The 2024 Cal Water Bear Gulch report states that all water served to its customers was purchased from SFPUC and reports an average hardness of 46 ppm. The SFPUC regional report describes chloramination and lists hardness ranging from 8.4 to 106 ppm with an average of 60 ppm, and says surface water made up all the water it supplied to regional customers in 2024.

Menlo Park Municipal Water, O'Connor Tract and Palo Alto Park Mutual Water Company each serve other addresses and publish their own reports. Check yours before you buy.

Source type: published water quality reports from California Water Service (Bear Gulch district, 2024) and the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission regional system.
