About Menlo Park 24/7 Plumbing

A local emergency line with licensed, insured plumbers behind it.

Menlo Park 24/7 Plumbing is a dispatch line at 1300 El Camino Real in Menlo Park, California. You call, a person answers, and we send a licensed and insured plumber to your door. The price is explained before work starts.

We built this site for the moment you need a plumber and have no time to compare ten websites. It answers the questions that come up on that call: what to shut off, who to phone, what the work involves and how the price is set.

How a Call Is Handled

A dispatcher asks where the water is, whether it is still running and what has already been shut off. We write that down so the plumber arrives with the right parts. Emergency calls are placed ahead of planned work.

The plumber assesses the problem on site and gives you a clear price. You decide whether to go ahead, and nothing changes without your approval.

What We Do Not Do

We do not handle electrical, heating and cooling or structural repairs, and we do not diagnose mold or asbestos. If a plumbing leak has damaged drywall or flooring, a plumber stops the water and you will need a separate contractor for the rebuild. We also do not promise an exact arrival time, because traffic on US-101 and El Camino Real changes it.

What to Expect When the Plumber Arrives

  1. Introduction and ID

    The plumber states the company name and why they are there. Ask to see a license number and look it up with the state board if you like.

  2. A look at the problem

    The plumber checks the fixture, the valve or the line and asks what you saw before the call.

  3. A clear price

    You get the price and the plan before anything is opened, cut or removed.

  4. The repair

    The work is done and the area is cleaned up. Wall or floor patching is a separate trade.

  5. Testing

    The plumber tests the repair for leaks, flow and pressure and shows you the result.

How We Decide What to Recommend

A fair plumber recommends the smallest fix that will last. A jammed disposal gets cleared, not replaced. A single pinhole leak in sound pipe gets repaired, not a whole-house repipe. A tank that is leaking from the body gets replaced, because no repair holds.

We say when a job is outside what we do, such as electrical work, mold testing or a rebuild after water damage. We would rather tell you that on the phone than after a truck is on the way.

Local Knowledge Behind the Call

Menlo Park has several water providers, including Menlo Park Municipal Water, California Water Service and O'Connor Tract Co-operative Water Company, and three sewer providers, with West Bay Sanitary District serving most of the city. Knowing which one applies to your street saves a phone call during an emergency.

Planning records put peak home building in Belle Haven in the 1950s and in Allied Arts between 1926 and 1940, so the age of the pipes behind your walls is not a mystery. On December 31, 2022, creek flooding put El Camino Real, Alma Street, Laurel Street and Middlefield Road under surface water, and that history shapes how we think about drains and sewer laterals.

Questions to Ask Any Plumber Before You Hire

A short list worth keeping

  • Can I see your contractor license number, and may I verify it with the state board.
  • Will you explain the price before work begins and put it in writing.
  • Does the price include parts, permits and cleanup.
  • What warranty comes with the repair.
  • Who will be in my home, and will they be on time.

Licensing and How to Check It

California requires plumbing contractors to hold a license from the Contractors State License Board. You can check any contractor at cslb.ca.gov or 1-800-321-2752 before you let them in. We encourage that step for every job, whoever you hire.

Standards the Trade Follows

Plumbing work in California is regulated by the state. Permit rules differ by city, so for a water heater or a repipe we point you to the Menlo Park Building Division at 650-330-6704 or the building department in your own town.
