Sewer Camera Inspection in Menlo Park, CA

A recorded look inside the pipe takes the guesswork out of repairs and home purchases.

A sewer camera inspection in Menlo Park, CA sends a small waterproof camera on a flexible rod through the sewer line from a cleanout to the street connection. The plumber watches a live view, notes the pipe material and records any roots, cracks, offsets, bellies or breaks. You get the footage and a plain explanation.

Inspection helps in three situations: repeat backups, a home purchase and any planned repair. Call (650) 860-1670 to schedule one. If sewage is backing up now, we clear the line first.

What the Camera Looks For

Roots

Fine hairs at joints mean an early problem. A thick mat means active intrusion.

Cracks and breaks

Longitudinal or circumferential cracks show up as dark lines on the pipe wall.

Offsets

Joints that have shifted allow debris to catch and roots to enter.

Bellies

Low spots where water sits and solids settle.

Scale and buildup

Rough, thickened walls reduce flow and catch waste.

How an Inspection Goes

  1. Find access

    The plumber locates a cleanout or pulls a fixture.

  2. Run the camera

    The camera moves toward the main, with footage recorded.

  3. Mark the spots

    A locator tool shows depth and position of any defects above ground.

  4. Review together

    You see the footage, hear what it means and get the recording.

Homebuyers: Why the Lateral Matters

A general home inspection does not look inside the sewer line. Older Menlo Park neighborhoods such as Allied Arts, Felton Gables, Belle Haven and the Willows have laterals from the 1930s to the 1950s, and a camera is the only way to see their condition. A pipe replacement can be a large expense, so knowing before closing is useful.

Ask the seller for any past camera footage and check with the sanitary district or the city about any lateral requirements tied to a sale. Rules differ by district.

Cleaning First, Camera Second

A pipe full of waste cannot be inspected. The plumber may need to clear the line first with a cable or a jet. That step belongs in the price, and the plumber tells you before it starts.

If your line is only slow, a sewer line cleaning visit with a camera at the end can be the most efficient plan.

Reading the Footage Together

Each defect gets a plain label and a rough position along the pipe. A hairline crack and a joint with a few roots are watch items, while a dropped section holding water is a repair item. The plumber separates what needs action now from what you can monitor.

Ask for the footage and a short written summary. Both are useful if you sell the house, file an insurance claim or compare repair prices from other contractors.
