DIY Leak Detection for Menlo Park, CA Homes

Three checks, in order, will tell you whether a leak is real and where to look first.

By the Menlo Park 24/7 Plumbing team · 2026

Many leaks can be found with a water meter, some food coloring and a careful look around. DIY leak detection for Menlo Park, CA homes follows a simple order: confirm there is a leak with the meter, rule out the cheap suspects such as toilets and irrigation, then narrow down the area.

If the meter keeps moving and you cannot find water, or if water is visible and damaging a wall, call (650) 860-1670. A plumber has tools that find leaks under floors and in walls without tearing them open.

Step One: The Meter Test

  1. Stop all water use

    Close faucets, stop the dishwasher and washer and make sure no one flushes.

  2. Find the meter

    Look for a box near the street or the front of the property. Open the lid carefully and check for insects.

  3. Read the dial

    Note the leak indicator, a small dial or triangle that spins with flow, and the main numbers.

  4. Wait fifteen to thirty minutes

    Do not use water. Check the dial again.

  5. Interpret the result

    If the indicator spins or the numbers change, water is leaving the system.

Step Three: Narrow Down the Zone

Close the main valve at the house and watch the meter. If the dial stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the house valve, in the service line. Both are useful clues.

To narrow down inside the house, close individual fixture shutoffs and see whether the meter stops. A hot water leak can be tested by closing the cold supply to the water heater.

Signs to Look For Around the House

Stains

Brown or yellow marks on a ceiling or wall that grow over time.

Musty smell

A smell without visible water can mean moisture inside a wall.

Warm floor

A warm spot may be a hot water line leaking under a slab.

Bubbling or cracked paint

Moisture behind the surface.

Sound of water

A hiss or trickle when everything is off.

When to Stop and Call

  • The meter moves with everything off and you cannot find the source.
  • A floor feels warm or a section of flooring is lifting.
  • You see water on a ceiling or in a wall.
  • A pipe or fitting is cracked or bursting.
  • You find a leak but cannot reach or shut it off.

What a Plumber Adds

Pressure testing isolates the supply lines. Acoustic and thermal tools find leaks under floors and behind walls, so cuts are small. A camera shows drain and sewer leaks from the inside. Our leak detection page explains each tool.

If the break turns out to be a split pipe, see burst pipe repair for what happens next. Photograph everything for your insurer.

Using Your Senses Around the House

Walk through slowly and put your hand on walls beneath sinks and near the water heater. A cool, damp patch on drywall suggests moisture inside. Look at ceilings below bathrooms and upstairs laundry rooms for stains. Check baseboards for swelling and carpet edges for dampness.

Listen in a quiet moment, such as late evening, with every fixture off. A faint hiss or tick can be a pressurized leak. Put your ear to the wall near the water heater and the main valve.

Record What You Find

Write down the meter reading, the time and what you tried. Take photos of stains and of the meter. A plumber can use that record to focus on the likely area, and an insurer may ask for it later.

If you suspect a leak under a slab, mention any warm floor spots, since that points toward a hot water line.
